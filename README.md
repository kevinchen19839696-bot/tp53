### 1. 【The LEGO Assembly Line】
**Question:**
What type of bond links amino acids together to form a protein? Which amino acids form hydrogen bonds with each other?

**Answer:**
#### Peptide Bonds
Amino acids are linked together by peptide bonds (a type of covalent amide bond). These bonds are formed through a condensation (dehydration synthesis) reaction between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amino group of the next, releasing a molecule of water in the process.

### 2. 【The Double-Track Highway of Life’s Blueprint】
**Question:**
How do nucleotides assemble to form double-stranded DNA? What are the basic structural components of DNA? Is DNA right-handed or left-handed? List the names of the four DNA bases. Which bases form hydrogen bonds with each other?

**Answer:**
#### Basic Structural Components of DNA
DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) is a polymer made up of repeating units called nucleotides. Each individual nucleotide consists of three parts:
* **A 5-carbon sugar:** Deoxyribose.
* **A phosphate group:** Attached to the 5' carbon of the sugar.
* **A nitrogenous base:** Attached to the 1' carbon of the sugar (one of four types: Adenine, Thymine, Cytosine, Guanine).

### 3. 【Searching for My One-and-Only Soulmate in the Universe: A 20-Base-Pair Love Code Hidden Among 3 Billion DNA Base Pairs】
**Question:**
The source of molecular stability and specificity: hydrogen bonding — How do TP53 and DNA recognize each other?
Using the structure 3KMD, identify one pair of an amino acid and a DNA base that interact with each other.

**Answer:**
#### Key Amino Acid and DNA Base Interaction
* **Amino Acid:** Arginine 280 (Arg280) located in helix H2
* **DNA Base:** Guanine (G) within the major groove of the consensus binding sequence

#### Nature of the Interaction
Arg280 acts as a crucial anchor for sequence-specific recognition. Its side chain reaches into the major groove of the DNA double helix and forms stable hydrogen bonds with the oxygen and nitrogen atoms (specifically the O6 and N7 positions) of a conserved guanine base. This direct read-out provides sequence specificity for TP53 recognition.

### 5. 【Return to the Crime Scene: Searching for Clues】
**Question:**
Using the TP53–DNA structure (3KMD), identify one TP53 amino acid mutation that can lead to uncontrolled cell division and contribute to the development of cancer.
Then examine this amino acid in ChimeraX. Based on its three-dimensional position, determine whether the mutated amino acid is located directly at the protein–DNA interface or somewhere else in the protein.

**Answer:**
#### 3D Position Analysis (Structure 3KMD)
When examining this residue in the 3KMD crystal structure using visualization software like ChimeraX:
* **Location:** Directly at the protein–DNA interface.
* **Structural Context:** Arg273 is classified as a classic "contact mutant" residue. Its side chain is positioned right at the binding surface where the p53 core domain meets the DNA double helix. In the wild-type protein, this arginine makes critical direct contacts (hydrogen bonds and electrostatic interactions) with the DNA bases and backbone within the major groove.
* **Effect of Mutation:** Replacing the long, positively charged arginine side chain with a smaller, uncharged histidine disrupts these precise interface contacts, severing the molecular anchor that locks p53 onto its DNA response element.
